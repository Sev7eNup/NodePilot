# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

NodePilot is a small, open project. Everyone taking part in it — reporting a bug, opening a pull
request, asking a question, answering one — is entitled to be treated with respect, regardless of
experience level, background, identity, or the language they are most comfortable in.

## Expected behaviour

- **Be direct and be kind.** Technical disagreement is welcome and useful; contempt is not.
  Criticise the code, the design, or the argument — not the person who brought it.
- **Assume competence and good faith.** A question that looks obvious to you usually means the
  documentation failed, not that the asker did.
- **Write for people who are not in your head.** Enough context that someone else can reproduce,
  verify, or disagree with what you said.
- **English is the working language on GitHub** — issues, pull requests, and reviews. It is
  nobody's grade that gets judged here, only whether the point comes across. Imperfect English is
  entirely fine; correcting someone's grammar unasked is not.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks, in public or in private.
- Discriminatory language or imagery, including sexualised language, and unwelcome attention of
  any kind.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption of discussions, or deliberately derailing threads.
- Encouraging or excusing any of the above.

## Security reports are separate

Do **not** open a public issue for a vulnerability. [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md) describes the
private reporting path. Reporting a security problem in good faith — even a mistaken one — is
always welcome and will never be held against you.

## Scope

This applies in every project space: issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, code
review, and the project's documentation. It also applies when someone is representing the project
in public elsewhere.

## Enforcement

Report anything that concerns you to **florian.kemper.ms@gmail.com**. Reports are handled
confidentially and you will get an acknowledgement.

This is a one-maintainer project, so there is no committee and no appeals board — the maintainer
decides. Depending on severity, the response ranges from a private word, through editing or
removing the offending content, to blocking an account from the repository. The reasoning will be
stated to the person concerned.

The maintainer is not exempt from this document.
